# CarePath Case Study

## Overview
CarePath is a mental-health access and operations concept designed to help users navigate support options while helping service teams identify demand patterns, wait-time bottlenecks, urgency levels, and follow-up gaps.

## Problem
People seeking mental health support often do not know where to start, what level of care they need, or how urgent their situation is. At the same time, behavioral health systems need better visibility into demand, wait times, referral completion, and access barriers.

## User Journey

The CarePath journey begins with a user who feels overwhelmed and does not know where to start. The user opens CarePath, answers a brief intake form, receives a general urgency/support category, and is routed toward a recommended next step such as an appointment, crisis resource, peer support, telehealth option, or follow-up.

The system then tracks whether the referral was completed, delayed, abandoned, or requires follow-up.

## Non-Diagnostic Triage Framework

CarePath uses a non-diagnostic routing framework to help users identify general support pathways based on urgency, access barriers, and preferred support type.

The framework includes three broad support levels:

### Low Urgency
Users reporting general stress, mild anxiety, or low immediate support needs may be routed toward self-guided resources, peer support, routine appointments, or digital check-ins.

### Moderate Urgency
Users reporting persistent distress, functional difficulty, limited support, or worsening symptoms may be routed toward outpatient therapy, telehealth, community behavioral health referrals, or care navigator follow-up.

### High Urgency
Users reporting crisis indicators, safety concerns, severe distress, or urgent support needs may be routed toward crisis lines, mobile crisis support, urgent behavioral health centers, or emergency support.

This framework is not intended to diagnose users. It is designed to reduce confusion, support access navigation, and help service teams understand demand patterns and bottlenecks.

## User Problem

People experiencing mental health distress often do not know where to start, what level of support they need, or how urgent their situation is.

## System Problem

Behavioral health providers often lack clean visibility into demand, urgency, wait times, referral completion, and where users drop off before receiving support.

## Goal
Design a non-diagnostic access and triage concept that helps users identify next steps while giving service teams operational insight.

## Deliverables
- User journey map
- Triage routing framework
- Mock intake dataset
- Operations dashboard
- Product and process recommendations

## Tools
GitHub, Python, CSV, dashboarding tool, and design/wireframing tools.

## Mock Dataset

To support the operations dashboard, I generated a synthetic intake dataset with 1,000 records. Each record represents a fictional CarePath user moving through the access and triage flow.

The dataset includes user demographics, presenting need, urgency level, access barriers, recommended support pathway, referral status, wait time, follow-up completion, and no-show risk.

This dataset is fully synthetic and does not contain real patient, client, or protected health information.
